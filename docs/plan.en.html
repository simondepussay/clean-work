Shukatsu Pilot — project plan

An iOS + web app for international students job hunting in Japan: application tracking, résumé builder, AI entry-sheet review and interview practice.

1. Overview

Who is it for? International students job hunting in Japan. Résumé formats, keigo in entry sheets and interviews in Japanese are major hurdles.

What does it do? Application tracking, JIS-format résumé PDFs, AI entry-sheet review (explanations in French and English too) and, in v1.1, spoken interview practice.

By when? 2027-03-31 (MVP live before summer-internship applications open (April 2027)). Development starts 2026-10-19.

Estimation assumptions: Solo developer alongside school (10–12 h/week). Velocity 10 SP per sprint → 1 SP ≈ 1.4 days. Story points use the Fibonacci scale (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13).

Practice questions (from this plan)

Q1. What is the critical path and the shortest total duration?

A → B → C → G → J → K → L → M (including dummy d1). Shortest duration: 82 days. Follow the events whose earliest and latest times are equal.

Q2. If activity E (Résumé PDF) is 5 days late, how late is the whole project?

E has 4 days of total float, so 5 − 4 = 1 day(s) late. A delay within the float doesn't affect the end date.

Q3. What is the total float of activity D (Application tracker)?

Latest start 32 − earliest start 21 = 11 days.

Q4. Why is dummy activity d1 (6 → 7) needed?

Entry-sheet review (G) and résumé PDF (E) both start at event 4 and end at event 7. In an arrow diagram two activities can't share the same pair of events, so event 6 is added and joined with a 0-day dummy.

Q5. PERT says 82 days — why is the Gantt chart longer?

PERT assumes parallel activities run at the same time (unlimited people). With one developer, development activities must be done one by one (resource levelling, 山崩し), which gives 120 days.
